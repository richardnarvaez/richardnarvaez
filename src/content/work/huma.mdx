## The problem

Vocabulary apps drill words out of context. People memorize lists, forget them a week later and quit.

Huma teaches words **inside a story**: a legend from a real place, told with illustrators from different countries and, alongside them, video and AI-made pieces. Each new word appears where it matters.

## Key decisions

<Decision
  title="Native SwiftUI instead of cross-platform"
  why="Reading and audio have to feel instant. Native gave the smoothest text rendering and offline playback with the least code."
  tradeoff="Android has to wait."
/>

<Decision
  title="Convex as the backend"
  why="Realtime sync of progress between devices came for free, and the schema lives next to the client code."
  tradeoff="A smaller ecosystem than Postgres; I keep the data model simple on purpose."
/>

<Decision
  title="Stories before features"
  why="Every release ships at least one new legend. The content is the product; features follow what readers ask for."
/>

<Decision
  title="Illustrators from each place, not one house style"
  why="A legend from Ecuador should look Ecuadorian. Local artists bring that; video and AI-made pieces fill the gaps and keep the release pace."
  tradeoff="Less visual uniformity across stories, more coordination."
/>

## What I learned

Short, illustrated chapters beat long lessons. The first version had ten-minute stories; nobody finished them. Two-minute chapters doubled completion.
